# Demo video: script and shot list

**Length:** about 9½ minutes, under the 10-minute limit.
**Use cases shown:** six. The brief asks for at least three, including the agentic feature. The three to give most time: the agent turning an idea into a change (use case 3), the agent as an AI developer (use case 4), and daily results (use case 6).
**Record on the online demo:** https://nickwg113.github.io/shopfix-au/. Everything in it is mocked, so it needs no API credit and can't go wrong on the day. You can type your own requests to the agent; docs/github-pages.md lists the kinds of request the mock understands. Press × on the purple "Online demo" banner if you don't want it in shot.

Before recording:

- Run each store through once, so pages load quickly on camera.
- Use browser zoom at 110–125% so text is readable on a laptop screen. Hide bookmarks. Close other tabs.
- Keys switch pages while you present: 1 home, 2 What to fix, 3 Preview and approve, 4 Results. Or click the steps at the top of each page.

---

## 0:00 Opening (30 s)

**Show:** the QuickLift home page.

**Say:** "Small Australian Shopify stores lose sales to things a shopper notices in seconds: no Afterpay near the price, delivery cost hidden until checkout, no returns policy in sight. They don't have a developer or enough traffic to run A/B tests. QuickLift finds those gaps on a store's real pages and fixes them, with a preview the owner approves first. You can try a recorded copy yourself at the link in our deck."

---

## Use case 1: check a store (1 min 30 s)

**Show:** the home page. Paste `frankbody.com` and press Check my store free. Step 1, **What to fix**, loads.

**Say and point:**

- Point at the step bar at the top: **1 What to fix → 2 Preview and approve → 3 Results**. **Say:** "Three steps, and the owner always knows which one they're on."
- The sentence under the store's name: "We found 3 things to fix. 4 are already looking good."
- "It reads only public pages: the home page, the product feed and one product page. No login or app install is needed to get a first answer."
- "What to fix first" names each problem, most important first.
- Open "Already looking good" to show what passed.
- "When it can't read something, like a hidden product feed, it says *Can't tell*. It never claims a store is missing something it couldn't see."

---

## Use case 2: one-click fixes on the real page (2 min)

**Show:** click a Fix item so the preview opens on the store's real page, with the **Fixes** tab open on the right.

1. Switch on **Afterpay instalments**. The page scrolls to the price, where "or 4 payments of …" appears. **Say:** "It's worked out from the sale price, not the crossed-out one."
2. Switch on the **Returns + Australian business bar**, then edit its wording in the box that appears under it. **Say:** "Only the owner knows what's true about their business, so every line can be changed."
3. Switch on **Sticky Add to cart**. It moves to Phone view and a product page. Scroll past Add to cart and the bar slides in.
4. Point at the violet "Changed by QuickLift" outlines. **Say:** "Every change is labelled, so the owner sees exactly what's different."

---

## Use case 3: an idea, then the agent makes it (1 min 15 s)

The agentic feature, part one. Take your time.

- On a product page, open the **Ideas** tab on the right. **Say:** "The agent looks at this exact page the way a first-time shopper on a phone would, and suggests changes, each one naming a real button or section on this page."
- Read one idea aloud, then switch it on. **The agent at work** opens right above the ideas and, a moment later, the change is on the page under **With your changes**, beside **Now**.
- **Say:** "One click from an idea to the change on the store's own page. Nothing is live yet."

---

## Use case 4: the agent as an AI developer (1 min 45 s)

The agentic feature, part two: the owner asks for something in their own words, and the agent does a developer's job.

- Open the **Ask the agent** tab and type, for example: *"Make the Add to cart button bright green, and add a small line under it saying Ships from Sydney in 1–2 business days."* Press **Make this change**.
- Point at **The agent at work** as it goes. **Say:** "It reads the page, finds what to change in the store's own theme, these class names here, and writes the CSS and JavaScript. You're watching it write the code."
- When it finishes, the page scrolls to the change. **Say:** "No developer, no ticket, no waiting a week. And it works to rules: it never invents reviews, discounts, countdown timers or claims about the business."
- Prove the rule: press the chip **Fake 5-star reviews (it’ll refuse)**. The log stops with a **!** and the reason, in red. **Say:** "It refuses, and explains why: fake reviews break Australian Consumer Law."

---

## Use case 5: approve, publish and undo (1 min)

The online demo acts this out: nothing is written to the real store, but every step shows.

- Point at **Your changes** at the top right: every change is listed on one line, each with **×** to remove it. **Say:** "Nothing goes live until the owner approves it."
- Press **Approve and publish**. The message says the changes are now live in one snippet in the store's theme.
- Press **Undo published changes**. **Say:** "All our code lives in one snippet, and Undo empties it. It never rewrites the owner's theme, so it can't wipe out their own edits." Then approve again.
- **Next: see the results →** appears under the button. That's use case 6.

In the full app with a dev store connected, this writes to the real theme: reload the store in another tab to show it live, then Undo.

---

## Use case 6: daily results (1 min 15 s)

Press **Next: see the results →** after approving, or **3 Results** in the step bar.

- **Say first:** "These are sample figures, labelled on the page, for a store making about A$100,000 a year. Connected to a real store, this page reads each day's visits, orders and revenue from Shopify."
- Read the first sentence aloud: revenue up since the changes went live.
- The three tiles: revenue for the 14 days since, the share of visits that bought, and today so far.
- Hover over the revenue chart: lighter bars before, violet after, and the dashed line where the changes went live. **Say:** "Compare weeks, not days: a small store's daily figures jump around."
- Point at **What went live**, and open **See every day** for the table. **Say:** "A 5% lift on A$100,000 a year is about A$5,000 more in sales, with no extra ad spend, and the owner can watch it day by day."

If time is short, cut use case 1's "Already looking good" and use case 2's returns-bar edit before cutting any of this.

Optional, 20 s, if there's time: check `culturekings.com.au`, a big brand where most checks pass. **Say:** "That's what good looks like, and the tool says so rather than inventing problems."
